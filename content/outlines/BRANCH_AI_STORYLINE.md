## Narrative goal

Present Branch AI as a product-systems case study rather than a collection of AI experiments. A recruiter should understand the product-team problem, Olumide's role, the three systems, and the strongest outcomes before deciding whether to continue. The story should carry Charlie Hart's calm seniority, Lucas Barrientos's narrative tension, and Olumide's confirmed first-person ownership.

The Substack article informs the rhythm—outcome, broken status quo, repeated failures, system architecture, pipeline, iteration, and future potential—but none of its prose should be copied.

## Recommended case title

> Building governed AI workflows for Branch's product team

## Recommended subtitle

> How I created reusable systems for on-brand illustrations, market-aware product copy, and Neem-based prototyping across India, Kenya, and Nigeria.

## Recommended summary

> Branch's product team was already using AI, but every person brought different context, prompts, and quality standards. I built the core systems behind three governed workflows for illustration, product copy, and prototyping, working with the design team to translate Branch's product, market, and visual rules into tools the wider team could reuse.

## Story order

### Opening — The output finally looked like one product

Purpose: begin with the changed state rather than the process.

- Show the shipped illustration system across live product markets.
- Establish that this was one visible result of a wider programme covering illustration, copy, and prototyping.
- Introduce the central tension: speed was already available; consistency was not.
- Present the outcome snapshot without the rejected usage claim.

### Executive summary — The team did not need another prompt

- AI adoption already existed across the product team.
- A team poll showed ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini were already part of copy workflows; do not attribute the poll to Olumide.
- Outputs lacked shared product context, market language, visual rules, component logic, and quality standards.
- Olumide reframed the opportunity as product infrastructure rather than prompt optimisation.
- State personal and team ownership boundaries immediately.

## Phase 1 — Why AI outputs kept drifting

### Fast outputs. Different versions of Branch.

Organise the repeated failures across the three workflows:

#### Illustration

- Mixed visual styles made the product feel fragmented.
- Colour, materials, lighting, perspective, distribution, and states drifted between generations.
- Other designers helped identify the consistency problem.

#### Product copy

- Generic AI did not know which products existed in each market.
- It missed local terminology, Branch's voice, and component-specific behaviour.
- A polished sentence could still be wrong for the product moment.

#### Prototyping

- Generic prototypes ignored Neem components and Branch interaction patterns.
- They were useful as rough sketches but weak as stakeholder-alignment tools.

Close the phase with the turning point:

> Better prompts could improve one output. Branch needed systems that could improve the next output, for the next person, in the next market.

This line is a proposed narrative direction, not approved final copy.

- Make human review a core part of the architecture rather than a disclaimer.
- Introduce the shared principle: encode context once, then make it reusable.

## Phase 3 — Three workflows in practice

This is the longest phase. Give Illustration GPT the deepest treatment.

### Workflow 01 — Teaching AI a visual language

#### Aligning on the direction

- Other designers helped surface the inconsistency problem.
- Olumide and Arushi explored the visual direction.
- Olumide led three public review sessions.
- Arushi ran the FigJam vote.
- The design team defined the colour system.

#### Building the four-layer system

Present the architecture as four responsibilities:

1. Instructions — system behaviour and non-negotiables; written by Olumide.
2. Manifest — operating logic and routing; built by Olumide.
3. Rules — style, colour, camera, and prompt contracts; JSON rules written by Olumide, with the colour system defined by the design team.
4. Materials — 15 surface recipes; catalogue built by Olumide.

Do not use the name `Veela`.

#### Two modes, one pipeline

- Render Mode starts from a reference image.
- Ideation Mode begins with structured concepts.
- Both enter the same render pipeline.
- The implemented material-realism gate can rerender twice before escalating to a person.

#### Refining the system

- Six major documented versions.
- More than 500 image-generation iterations, often 10–15 per final illustration.
- Olumide generated and refined the illustrations.
- The design team owned quality review and final selection.
- All product markets are live.
- Do not state an exact illustration count.

### Workflow 02 — Turning product context into reusable copy rules

#### What generic AI missed

- Product truth
- Market terminology
- Brand voice
- Component context
- Character, casing, and lint constraints

#### Building and tuning CopyGPT

- The team defined brand and market rules.
- Olumide owned component and lint rules and version tuning.
- The system progressed through seven versions.
- Human review measured accuracy moving from 95% to 99%.
- The screen and component audit was a team workflow, not sole ownership.
- The product manager led rollout across India and African markets.

#### Measured work

- 530 screens reviewed
- 4,390 components audited
- 1,778 changes implemented by the product team
- Preserve the limitation: India totals were audited while Kenya and Nigeria included directional estimates.

### Workflow 03 — Making PRDs tangible sooner

#### What generic prototypes missed

- Neem components
- Branch UX patterns
- Token compliance
- Product-flow conventions

#### The workflow Olumide created

- PRD or product idea
- Claude Skill interpretation
- Figma MCP and Neem context
- Token-compliant React prototype
- Stakeholder review
- PRD refinement before high-fidelity design

#### Validation

- Product managers, designers, and developers tested the workflow.
- Directional product-manager feedback suggested about 30% faster PRD evaluation.
- Stakeholders could react to an interaction instead of only a written description.

## Phase 4 — Testing with the product team

### Governance did not remove judgment

- Show how feedback became reusable rules.
- Explain which decisions remained human: storytelling, abstraction, inclusivity, context, and final approval.
- Contrast individual prompting with shared operating systems.
- Do not imply autonomous production without review.

### Iterations and abandoned paths

- Show the illustration directions that failed and why they were abandoned.
- Annotate the colour, material, camera, or consistency issue visible in each example.
- Show how repeated failures became rules or negative constraints.
- For CopyGPT, show how over-strict and under-specified rules produced different failure modes.
- For prototyping, contrast a generic output with one that correctly used Neem patterns.
- Include only iterations that changed the system; do not publish an exhaustive process archive.

### Team roles

- Designers owned colour definition and final illustration review.
- The product team implemented approved copy changes.
- The product manager owned CopyGPT rollout.
- Cross-functional users tested the prototyping workflow.
- Deovrat supported alignment through one-to-one check-ins.

### Recognition

Use Deovrat's approved comment as the only recommendation inside the case. Keep the quote separate from evidence and ownership claims.

## Phase 5 — Outcomes and hindsight

### Outcomes

- A shared illustration system is live across all product markets.
- Copy review moved from generic rewriting toward product, market, component, and lint validation.
- Interactive prototypes made PRD feedback more concrete and directionally faster.
- The team had reusable context instead of rebuilding it in every prompt.

### What Olumide learned

Proposed learning themes:

- AI quality depends more on context architecture than on one clever prompt.
- Guardrails need active tuning: too few produce drift; too many produce rigidity.
- Internal AI tools need the same product thinking as customer-facing tools.
- Human judgment becomes more valuable when repetitive corrections become reusable rules.
- Ownership must remain visible when systems are built collaboratively.

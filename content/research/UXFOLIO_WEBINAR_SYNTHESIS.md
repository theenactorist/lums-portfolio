# UXfolio Case-Study Webinar Synthesis

Status: Adopted into Theme 2 editorial rules
Last updated: 2026-06-27

## Core interpretation

The portfolio is a product. The recruiter or hiring manager is a user trying to assess role fit quickly. Every card, heading, visual, and section should reduce the effort required to make that decision.

This complements the existing Theme 2 direction:

- Charlie Hart supplies calm seniority and editorial presentation.
- Lucas Barrientos supplies expressive, evidence-led storytelling.
- The webinar supplies recruiter-first information architecture and scanning discipline.
- Olumide's approved fact sheets supply truth, ownership, and attribution.

## Adopt directly

### Recruiter-first hierarchy

- Put the most role-relevant case first.
- Assume many visitors will scan only cards, titles, the hero, the TLDR, and headings.
- Surface role, year, platform, scope, and strongest outcomes early.
- Treat every level as a decision point to continue or leave.

### Informative cards and titles

- Replace bare product names or vague slogans with titles that explain what was designed and for whom.
- Add a subtitle carrying scope, impact, audience, or the product problem.
- Show one or two strong, readable product visuals.
- Keep card composition consistent across all projects.

### Short intro and visible outcomes

- Lead with a TLDR instead of a long company introduction.
- Pair the problem with a preview of the solution.
- Place high-value metrics or qualitative outcomes near the top.
- Preserve detailed context for visitors who continue.

### Decision-focused process

- Show the moments that changed the solution.
- Include failed or abandoned directions and explain why they stopped.
- Exclude routine process steps that do not demonstrate judgment.
- Connect evidence to insight, decision, and consequence.

### Contextual visuals

- Annotate screens and prototypes.
- Explain before/after differences.
- Crop workshops, surveys, and reports to the relevant insight.
- Avoid unreadable zoomed-out artefacts.
- Use interactivity when it helps the reader understand behaviour.

### Outcomes and reflection

- Use quantitative evidence where it is verified.
- Use qualitative evidence when metrics are unavailable or confidential.
- Close with what changed and what Olumide learned. A “what I would do differently” reflection is optional and should appear only when it adds something specific and owner-confirmed.
- Link to the next relevant case study.

## Adapt rather than enforce literally

### 70% visual / 30% text

Use this as a section-level heuristic for evidence-heavy moments, not a fixed page-wide quota. Some architecture or strategy sections need more explanation; final-design sections can be more visual.

### Suggested case-study structure

Use the webinar's intro, problem/solution, research, process/iterations, testing, outcomes, and final-design sequence as a coverage check. Keep project-specific phases and labels so leadership, systems, and product cases do not feel identical.

### Interactivity

Use embedded prototypes or recordings only where interaction communicates something a static image cannot. Preserve performance and accessibility alternatives.

### Metrics

Never add a metric simply because recruiters expect one. Use verified qualitative evidence and clearly state attribution limits when precise numbers are unavailable.

## Do not adopt

- A rigid template that makes every case study tell the same story.
- Decorative device mockups that reduce screen readability.
- Process artefacts used only to prove that a standard UX activity occurred.
- Unsupported role inflation, impact claims, or causation.
- Long company histories before the project premise.
- A fixed page length independent of the decisions the project needs to explain.

## Applied to Branch AI

- Approved card/hero title: `Turning fragmented AI use into consistent product outputs at Branch`.
- Subtitle: `How I built reusable workflows for on-brand illustrations, market-aware product copy, and Neem-based prototypes across India, Kenya, and Nigeria.`
- Place role, timeline, users, tools, and outcomes in a concise overview near the top.
- Organise the problem around three visible failure lanes: illustration, product copy, and prototyping.
- Give Illustration GPT the deepest visual and decision treatment.
- Show failed illustration directions and rule-tuning examples with annotations.
- Keep CopyGPT and Neem DS Prototyper compact but evidence-rich.
- Show outcomes early and return to attribution, learning, and future potential at the end.
